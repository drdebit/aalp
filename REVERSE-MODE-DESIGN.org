#+TITLE: Reverse mode — from the entry back to the assertions
#+AUTHOR: Matt DeAngelis, with Claude
#+DATE: 2026-10-06

Noted, not built. Raised by Matt on 2026-10-06 while reading the welcome
page draft: the platform teaches assertions → entry, and students will
spend the rest of 2101 reading entries written by other people. They
need the other direction too. This records the idea and the design
decisions so far, for after the pilot.

* The idea

The student writes a journal entry. The system does not grade it as an
entry; it reads it, and shows what the business would have had to
assert for that entry to fall out of the rulebook. Then it asks the
student to finish the description.

The point is dual fluency in the other direction. An entry is a lossy
summary of an event; the assertive record is richer. Reverse mode makes
the student feel the loss and repair it.

* Why the map is one-to-many, and why that is the lesson

Several assertion sets derive the same entry:

| entry                             | what it leaves unsaid                              |
|-----------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------|
| DR Cash / CR Common Stock         | nothing further: an owner put money in             |
| DR Equipment / CR Cash            | what the machine allows the business to do         |
| DR Finished Goods / CR Cash       | that the goods were bought to sell on as they are  |
| DR Raw Materials / CR Cash        | that the goods were bought to be used up making something |
| DR Interest Expense / CR Interest Payable | that the promise is to the lender          |
| DR Accounts Receivable / CR Revenue, DR COGS / CR Finished Goods | which batch the goods came out of; how sure the business is of being paid |

So the system cannot show /the/ assertions. It can show what the entry
*commits* the business to having said, and what it leaves open.

* The construction (Matt, 2026-10-06)

Map the entry to the assertion *pattern*, then ask the student to fill
in the gaps from the narrative.

- The accounts imply the skeleton. Equipment implies a capacity, so the
  system adds an =allows= to the sentence -- but leaves blank *what* the
  equipment allows, and the student fills that from the text. Raw
  Materials implies an =expects= to consume, blank as to what is made.
  Interest Payable implies a =requires= to a party, blank as to whom.
  Accounts Receivable implies a =requires= from the customer and an
  =expects= with a confidence the student has to judge.
- The student completes the sentence; the derivation runs forward on
  the completed sentence; it must reproduce the entry they started
  from. That is the check, and it needs no new grading machinery.
- This emphasises the meaning of the entry while keeping the fact that
  the assertive base is richer: the entry gave the skeleton, the record
  needed the rest.

* How it can be built cheaply

Everything needed is already in the codebase:

- =classification/classifications= maps each pattern to its entry.
  Inverting is a lookup: every pattern whose =:journal-entry= matches
  the student's accounts.
- =je_conformance/canonical-selection= already synthesises an assertion
  set from a pattern's spec (required assertions and their required
  parameters). Move it out of =test/= into the app.
- Intersect the candidates' assertion sets for what the entry commits
  to; diff them for what is open. Parameters the spec leaves =:any=
  (the item, the party, the confidence, the batch) are exactly the gaps
  to hand the student.
- Run each completed sentence back through =je-derive/derive-je= to
  confirm it reproduces the entry. All forty served patterns conform
  (oracle, 2026-10-05), so the inversion is exact over the course's
  vocabulary.
- The entry input exists: =je-constructor= in views.cljs (debit,
  credit, amount from dropdowns), built for the old "construct" mode.
  The old "reverse" problem type (entry shown, student picks
  assertions) is the same exercise without the gap-filling, and is only
  reachable from the free-practice selector; it should be retired into
  this.

* Two forms

1. *A problem type in each lesson's practice.* "Here is an entry a
   bookkeeper wrote for this event. Say what the business must have
   asserted, and finish what the entry could not say." The entry comes
   from the lesson's own patterns; the narrative is the usual one.
   Modest work once the inversion exists.
2. *An explorer on the sentence builder.* "Start from the entry": the
   student writes debits and credits, the sentence fills in with the
   skeleton and blanks, and they see what they have and have not said.
   For the student who thinks in entries and wants to translate.

* Open

- Which lessons get the problem type first. Cash Transactions (the
  purpose gap: raw materials versus finished goods) and Legal (the
  =allows= gap) make the point most sharply.
- Whether the explorer should also run outside a problem, as a place to
  type any 2101 entry and see its assertive reading.
- Amounts: the construct input takes one amount; a sale with cost of
  goods sold has two lines at two figures.
