#+TITLE: Reporting in the lesson path
#+AUTHOR: Matt DeAngelis (with Claude)
#+DATE: 2026-09-29

* The problem

The Report Builder (=REPORT-BUILDER-DESIGN.md=) composes reports over
/the student's own ledger/ and lives in Year 2. The pilot runs the
lessons instead ([[file:PILOT-LESSONS-PLAN.org][PILOT-LESSONS-PLAN.org]]), where problems come from
randomly chosen practice companies. There is no single business whose
books the student has kept, so there is nothing obvious to report on.

Matt's suggestion: run report queries over any one, or an assortment,
of the ledgers of the companies the student has worked with.

* What exists, and what does not

- The engine composes over *any list of events* (=engine/store-of=), so
  the Report Builder can run over a practice company's record as easily
  as over a student's ledger. The backend is not the constraint.
- The builder's frontend exists (composer in the sentence-builder idiom,
  read-only DSL mirror, preview, record, exemplar presets), reachable
  only in Year 2.
- *A practice company's record is regenerated for every problem.* About
  23 random draws set its prices, quantities, customers and loans, so
  "Northside Tees" is a different shop each time a student meets it.
  There is no stable company ledger today.
- *An attempt stores the student's assertions, but not which company it
  belonged to*, nor that company's record at the time.
- No lesson teaches reporting. The =reports= assertion (Level 5) is a
  recognition inside one entry, not a report over many events.

* What reporting should teach

From the Report Builder design, unchanged: the same events, walked
differently, give different reports, and the walk is itself a recorded,
accountable thing. Stage 1 /reads/ two finished compositions over the
same events (accrual revenue and cash revenue differ only in what they
exclude); Stage 2 /modifies/ one; Stage 3 /composes/ from blank and
records it.

That needs a ledger with enough in it to make the definitions disagree:
credit sales not yet collected, an advance not yet earned, an accrual.
By Adjusting Entries a student has met all of these.

* Options: whose ledger

| Option | Ledger | For | Against |
|--------+--------+-----+---------|
| A. Fixed company records | Each practice company has one full-year record, the same for every student | Comparable across students; answers knowable in advance; gradable by result | The student did not write any of it |
| B. Stable per student | A company's record is seeded per student, so Northside is the same shop each time *this* student meets it | Continuity without running a business | Answers differ per student; grading must compute them |
| C. The student's own entries | B's record plus every event the student recorded for that company, as recorded — errors kept | Reporting on books partly their own; a wrong entry shows up in the report, which is the period-close lesson without the Guided Year | Needs the company stored on each attempt; a report can be "wrong" because an entry was |
| D. An assortment | A query across several companies' ledgers at once | Analytics-style questions ("revenue across every shop you have kept") | Adds a dimension before the basic one is taught |

* Recommendation

1. *A reporting lesson*, after Adjusting Entries and before Equity. It
   needs sales, collections, advances and accruals to make the
   definitions disagree, and all of those are taught by then.
2. *Start from option A* for the lesson itself: one or two fixed
   company records, a full year each, the same for everyone. Stage 1
   and 2 run over them, and its practice round asks report questions
   ("What was Harbor Line's accrual revenue for the year?") graded by
   result against the canonical composition over the same ledger. Fixed
   records make answers knowable and the round gradable now.
3. *Build toward C* for the capstone: from now on, record the company on
   every attempt, and give each student a stable record per company
   (B). At the capstone, the student reports on a company whose books
   hold their own entries — and where a report comes out wrong because
   an entry was, that is the error archaeology the Guided Year was
   meant to deliver, reached without running a business.
4. *Leave D for later* (analytics courses, per the roadmap).

* What gross margin needs that the engine cannot do yet

Found 2026-09-29, reading =assertive-engine/compute/collects.clj= and
=aalp/engine.clj=.

1. *The engine's revenue exemplars are wrong for the current model.*
   =accrual-revenue-spec= collects events that provide goods AND receive
   cash, excluding =requires=: cash sales only, with credit sales — the
   case accrual accounting exists for — left out. =cash-revenue-spec=
   collects every cash receipt without an open promise, which takes in
   owner investment and borrowing. These are the Report Builder's
   Stage 1 exemplars; as they stand they teach the wrong answers.
2. *Revenue on credit is a promise, not a flow.* A composition sums one
   flow (=provides=, =receives=, =consumes=, =creates=); a credit sale's
   money is on =requires=.
3. *Cost of goods sold is derived, not asserted.* A sale asserts the
   shirts that went out; what they cost comes from the batch named, via
   the record's cost basis. No sum over assertions reaches it.
4. *No arithmetic across reports.* Gross margin is revenue less cost of
   goods sold: one report combining two recorded ones. The engine has
   sum, count and average over a single collection.
5. *No filter on which promise a =fulfills= keeps.* Cash-basis gross
   margin needs collections from customers and payments to suppliers,
   which are =fulfills= events distinguished only by the promise kept.

* Build plan (proposed)

1. *Engine: patterns and aggregates.* Pattern keys for "keeps a promise
   of kind K" and "has a counterparty"; aggregates for the money a
   =requires= promises, and for the cost of goods that went out (read
   from the cost basis, the way the derivation prices COGS).
2. *Engine: a report over reports.* A composition whose inputs are
   recorded reports and whose operation is difference (gross margin) —
   the research model's "calculations as events", recorded with its
   inputs so it can be traced.
3. *Correct the exemplars* to the current model, or retire them.
4. *Fixed records.* One or two practice companies with a full year: cash
   and credit sales, collections, cash and credit purchases, payments,
   an advance, production, so cash and accrual gross margin differ, and
   for more than one reason.
5. *Composition grading.* Compare each part — what is included, what is
   excluded, what is summed — against the canonical composition, with
   feedback per part. Accept a different composition when it collects
   the same events as the canonical one on the fixed record *and* on
   hidden test records built to separate near-misses (the design doc's
   "grading by extension"), so an equivalent form is not marked wrong.
6. *The lesson.* Tutorial and quiz; Stage 1 reads the two finished
   revenue compositions; Stage 2 modifies one; Stage 3 composes cost of
   goods sold, or a gross margin, from blank. Placed after Adjusting
   Entries.
7. *Later, for the capstone:* the company on every attempt, a stable
   record per student and company, a correction pass, then reporting.

* Decided: the two bases (2026-09-29)

- [X] *Cash basis follows one that exists: the tax cash method* for a
      small business holding inventory. After the 2017 act, a business
      under the gross-receipts test (IRC 448(c)) may use the cash method
      with inventory, treating it as non-incidental materials and
      supplies (471(c); Reg. 1.162-3): receipts count when received —
      cash sales, collections, advances — and the cost of goods is
      deducted in the year they are used or sold, *and only if paid
      for*; not when bought.
      *Confirmed against the sources, 2026-09-29:*
      - Reg. 1.471-1(b)(4): the cost of inventory treated as
        non-incidental materials and supplies is recovered "only in the
        taxable year in which the inventory is used or consumed in the
        taxpayer's business, or in the taxable year in which the taxpayer
        pays for or incurs the cost of the inventory, whichever is
        later." Its example: peanut butter bought in November, baked into
        cookies in December, cookies sold in January — deductible in
        January, when provided to customers.
      - The gross-receipts test (448(c)) for 2026 is average annual gross
        receipts of $32 million or less over the prior three years (Rev.
        Proc. 2025-32). Publication 538 (01/2022) still shows the older
        $26 million.
      - Receipts: "Under the cash method, you include in your gross
        income all items of income you actually or constructively
        received during the tax year" (Pub. 538) — so collections count
        when collected, and customer advances when received.
      Sources: [[https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/26/1.471-1][26 CFR 1.471-1]];
      [[https://www.irs.gov/publications/p538][IRS Publication 538]];
      [[https://www.currentfederaltaxdevelopments.com/blog/2025/10/9/2026-inflation-adjustments-for-tax-professionals-revenue-procedure-2025-32-analysis][Rev. Proc. 2025-32 analysis]].
      So cash gross margin = receipts from customers for goods, when
      received, less the cost of goods that went out during the period
      from batches that have been paid for.
      A plain "cash from customers less cash paid for goods" is a
      cash-flow measure, not a basis, and is not used.
- [X] *Accrual basis:* revenue from goods provided to customers, paid or
      promised, less the cost of the batches that went out. Cost of
      services would replace cost of goods for a company that provides
      services — but no practice company does: every practice sale is of
      shirts, and the one service-provider template went to 2102 with
      labour. Revisit if a service company is added.
- [X] *Composed from blank: cost of goods sold (accrual).* Students already
      name the batch when costing a sale; this is the same act, totalled
      over the year. Gross margin then combines it with a revenue report
      modified in Stage 2.

* What the cash basis adds to the build

- A way to tell whether a batch has been paid for: bought for cash, or
  bought on credit with the payable since settled (=fulfills=). The
  record holds both facts already.
- Cost of goods sold, cash basis, is then the accrual composition with
  one more exclusion — batches not yet paid for — which makes the pair a
  good Stage 2 exercise: one chip, a different basis.

* Progress

** Engine (2026-09-29) — done, uncommitted
Architecture decided: the engine gains generic powers; AALP supplies the
accounting readings as it loads events. Moving the readings into the
engine stays possible later.
- Several flows of one kind in one event (a production consuming shirts
  and ink) decompose and reconstitute.
- The AALP adapter keeps what reports need: =fulfills= names the promise
  kept (an edge to it); a flow of goods keeps the batch it came out of
  (=:draws-from=); =reports= keeps its amount; client readings are stored
  on the event (=:event/readings=).
- Patterns over readings (=:readings {:counterparty-role :customer}=, a
  set meaning any of), and totals over a reading (=:reading/goods-cost=,
  =:reading/consideration=) as money.
- A report over reports: =combine-and-record!= (=:difference=, =:sum=),
  recorded as an event whose targets are the reports it combines, from
  the figures they recorded rather than by re-running them.
- The old =accrual-revenue-spec= and =cash-revenue-spec= are documented
  as the paper's first pass (credit sales left out; owner investment let
  in) and kept, since the SP example's tests are written against them.
  AALP will not use them as presets.
- Tests: 56, 317 assertions, 0 failures (4 new, 21 assertions).

** The capstone's own year (2026-09-29) — built, uncommitted
Decided: a short year the student records, not practice answers grouped
by company (those rarely make a coherent year, so reports would fail for
reasons that are not the student's). Placed inside the Capstone, after
its mixed practice round.
- =capstone.clj=: Campus Threads' 2026 — three opening events given, then
  eleven transactions the student records (collections, credit and cash
  purchases and sales, a supplier payment, a cleaning service, an owner's
  draw). Correct books: accrual 2,870 / 1,420 / 1,450; cash 2,350 / 940 /
  1,410. Entries are graded attempts (problem type =capstone=; the latest
  counts), so no schema change.
- Recording: each transaction read against the student's own books so
  far; batches and promises chosen from what they recorded; no verdicts.
- Review: every entry judged against the right books up to its date, so
  each is judged on its own; any entry can be revised, with feedback.
- Report: the four reports and both margins over their own books, graded
  by composition; each correct report shows their figure beside the right
  books' and names the entries behind any difference — or, when the
  figures agree, names a wrong entry the report collected but does not
  total.
- Two classifications the teaching model lacked: =receivable-collection=
  and =payable-payment= (keeping a trade promise), graded like the other
  payments. *No lesson practises either* before the capstone.
- Fixed on the way: a customer collection could not find the promise it
  kept. The "received goods take their purpose from their own event"
  change had emptied the record for every line matched on =receives=,
  cash included; it now applies only to the line placing the goods.
- Walked through in a browser: all eleven recorded, two mistakes flagged,
  one revised with feedback, the other traced through the reports.
