#+TITLE: Selecting events and calculating from them
#+AUTHOR: Matt DeAngelis (with Claude)
#+DATE: 2026-10-09

The interface every report on the platform is built with: the Reporting
lesson's sales, cost of goods sold and ending inventory; each adjusting
entry; the capstone's reports; and, later, analytics over many records.
Asked for by [[file:REPORTS-AND-ADJUSTING.org][REPORTS-AND-ADJUSTING.org]] ("The selection interface").

Matt's requirement (2026-10-09): a robust interface for selecting events
according to assertive criteria, both including and excluding, and then
building calculations off them. Intuitive to students but powerful,
because the platform will need advanced querying and analytics later.
Grading nuanced and targeted to the specific choices students make and
how well they have made them, with excellent feedback.

* What exists, and what it already gets right

- *The Reporting composer* (=reporting.clj=, =report-composer= in
  =views.cljs=): a report as a sentence of five choices -- what the
  business did, whose, a paid-for condition, when, what is totalled --
  whitelisted into an engine collects-spec; a read-only DSL mirror
  beneath it; free preview, with the events collected marked in the
  record panel; grading part by part with feedback per part.
- *The Report Builder* (=REPORT-BUILDER-DESIGN.md=, Year 2): the fuller
  form -- include chips, exclude chips, an aggregate -- in the same
  sentence idiom, recorded as an event with provenance. The Reporting
  composer is a narrower reading of it. This design is the two of them
  joined, and it keeps the Builder's three decisions: sentence plus
  mirror, free preview, deliberate record.
- *The engine*: a collects-spec with includes and excludes, patterns
  over readings (counterparty role, batch paid), totals over readings
  (consideration, goods cost), a report over reports (difference, sum),
  and replaying a recorded report as recorded.
- *The sentence builder's pickers*: a batch for goods going out, a
  promise for =fulfills=. Each is the selection of one event from the
  record, and the model for "the prepayment of January 5".

What is missing is one vocabulary of criteria that all of these read
from, excludes in the lesson path, calculations that are shares rather
than totals, and grading that explains a selection by what it collected.

* The sentence

Three clauses. The first two select, the third calculates.

#+begin_example
Collect events where [criteria] , but not [criteria] ;
then [calculation] .
#+end_example

Each criterion is a chip chosen from a menu; a chip is a question the
record can answer about an event. The mirror beneath renders the same
thing as a query, read before anyone writes one. A report with no
excludes reads as the Reporting composer does today.

How each report reads in it:

| Report              | Collect                                                                | But not                            | Then                                                         |
|---------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------+------------------------------------+--------------------------------------------------------------|
| Sales revenue       | the business provides goods; the other party is a customer; in the period |                                    | total what was received or promised for them                 |
| Cash revenue        | the business receives money; from a customer; in the period            |                                    | total the money received                                     |
| Cost of goods sold  | the business provides goods; to a customer; in the period              | (cash basis: from unpaid batches)  | total what the goods cost                                    |
| Ending inventory    | the business receives goods; to the report date                        |                                    | total what they cost, less the same over goods provided      |
| Prepaid used        | money out for a service still to come: this prepayment                 |                                    | the share of its term that falls in the period, at its cost  |
| Depreciation        | goods received to use, not sell: this press                            |                                    | the share of its useful life in the period, of cost less salvage |
| Wage expense        | the business consumes labor hours; in the period                       |                                    | total hours at the hire's rate                               |
| Wages payable       | promises to pay employees; open at the report date                     |                                    | total what is promised                                       |
| Interest expense    | the note's interest promises; falling in the period                    |                                    | total what is promised                                       |
| Interest payable    | the note's interest promises; fallen due; open at the report date      |                                    | total what is promised                                       |
| Bad debts           | promises by customers to pay; open at the report date                  |                                    | total amount x (1 - confidence), less earlier reports         |

Two of these are differences of reports, like gross margin: ending
inventory (received less provided, both at cost, both to date) and the
allowance top-up (needed less reported before). The margin row already
built does this, and it is the right UI for them: a balance is "to
date", an income figure is "for the period", and a student who builds
ending inventory as two to-date reports has seen why.

The adjusting entry's journal entry is derived from the report: the
debit from what is reported (expense), the credit from what the selected
events are -- a prepayment gives Prepaid Expense, capital gives
Accumulated Depreciation, pay promises give Wages Payable, interest
promises give Interest Payable, customer promises give the Allowance.
The derivation reads the selection, not a =requires= in the same event.

* Criteria: the chips

Grouped by the question they ask. The vocabulary grows by lesson, the
way =unit-kind-level= gates kinds today; the groups are the menu's
headings.

1. *What the business did*: provides, receives, consumes, creates;
   promised something (=requires=); kept a promise (=fulfills=);
   reported.
2. *What moved*: money; goods (any, or a named item); a service; hours
   of labor; ownership units.
3. *Who*: the other party's role, read from the event -- customer,
   supplier, owner, lender, borrower, employees. Never a name.
4. *Which promise*: open at the report date; kept; falling due in the
   period; of a kind (to receive money, to provide goods, to pay
   employees, interest on a note).
5. *Where from*: drawn from a batch; allowed by an event (the press, the
   hire, the note); this event (one pick from the record, as the batch
   and promise pickers do now).
6. *When*: in the period; to the report date; before the period.
7. *Condition*: from batches that had been paid for (the tax cash basis).
8. *Reported before*: by an earlier report over the same events (later
   years).

Exclude chips are the same vocabulary after "but not". The Reporting
lesson's five parts are chips from groups 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7 with no
excludes; Adjusting Entries adds groups 4, 5 and 8 and the exclude
clause; later courses open the whole menu.

Readings are what make the chips possible: a chip asks about a reading
the record computes as it loads (=readings.clj=), so "a customer" is
read from what the event does, and "open at the report date" from
whether any later event =fulfills= the promise. New readings needed:
hours and the rate they draw on (from the hire the work event is allowed
by); whether a promise has fallen due by a date; which event a report
reported over.

* Calculations

The third clause. Four forms, and the calculation builder's four bases
fold into them.

- *A total* over a reading of the collected events: money received;
  money promised; what the goods cost; hours at the rate; amount x
  (1 - confidence). The Reporting composer's "total what" today.
- *A share*: of time -- from the event's date (or the period's start, if
  later) to the report date (or the term's end, if earlier), over the
  term; of life -- the same over the useful life, of cost less salvage.
  The dates and the term are read from the selected event; the student
  supplies only what the record does not hold, and the aim is for the
  record to hold it (salvage and useful life as terms of the acquisition,
  the way a note states its rate).
- *A difference* of two reports: gross margin, ending inventory, the
  allowance top-up. Built from recorded reports, never re-run
  (=combine-and-record!=).
- *Later*: count, average, group by, arbitrary periods -- the analytics
  forms; the engine has count and average already.

A later year is a full year's share, which equals the elapsed total less
what earlier reports took; the last year is the remainder. The builder
shows both and lets the student see they agree
(REPORTS-AND-ADJUSTING.org, "Earlier reports").

* The record panel

Always beside the sentence, every event on one line, the collected ones
marked, exactly as the Reporting round has it. A click on an event opens
it in the sentence builder's full reading. Later: a click could propose
criteria ("events like this one"), which is how analytics users explore;
not needed for the lessons.

* Recording a report

A report is an event in the record: a date, what it collected (the
include and exclude criteria), the calculation, the figure, and who
asserted it. In the flat map the sentence builder, grader and derivation
read:

#+begin_example
{:has-date {:date "2026-12-31"}
 :reports  {:category "expense" :basis "time-based" :amount 900
            :collects {:includes [...criteria...] :excludes [...]}
            :period {:from "2026-01-01" :to "2026-12-31"}}}
#+end_example

and in the full form (=canonical-edn=) as the research vocabulary has
it: =collects= / =includes= / =excludes= under the event, =reports= with
its calculation. Recorded reports are then events later reports can
select ("reported before"), which is what later years need; the
=recorded-report= entity already exists for the figure and its
provenance.

* Grading: by extension, then by part, then the figure

The composition is what is graded, and the feedback is computed from
what it collected rather than authored per task, so it scales from a
lesson to analytics.

1. *Extension first.* Run the student's selection and the canonical one
   over the record shown and over hidden records built to separate
   near-misses. Same events collected, same calculation: right, however
   it was composed. An equivalent form is never marked wrong.
2. *A different extension is explained by its difference.* For an event
   collected that should not be: name it, say what it is, and name the
   criterion that would leave it out -- "Shirts-002 is goods coming in,
   a purchase from a supplier; cost of goods sold collects goods going
   out, to customers." For an event missed: name it and the criterion
   that excludes it -- "Sale-004 is a sale on credit, and no money has
   come in; revenue on the accrual basis totals what was received /or
   promised/." Each message is built from the event's readings and the
   chip at fault, so it names the student's own choice.
3. *Same extension, wrong calculation*: feedback on the total or the
   share -- "you totalled what the customers paid; cost of goods sold
   totals what the goods cost"; "the share runs from January 5, when
   the cover began, not from the start of the year."
4. *The figure last*, and exactly: the record determines it, so a right
   selection and calculation with a wrong figure is arithmetic, and said
   so.
5. *Well-made, not only right.* A selection that collects the right
   events by a fragile route (a name where a role was meant; a date
   range that happens to fit) passes on the record shown and fails a
   hidden record, and the feedback says which record broke it and why.
   That is the nuance Matt asked for: the choice is graded, not the
   number.

The per-part feedback the Reporting grader has now
(=part-feedback=) becomes the fallback when the diff has nothing to
say, which is rare.

* What changes in Reporting (decided with Matt, 2026-10-09)

The composer is the student's first meeting with this interface, so it
is this interface, not a form of its own. Four things change from the
2026-09-29 version; the sentence with its mirror, the free preview with
the collected events marked, Harbor Line's year and the sequence of
tasks all stay.

1. *Chips, not slots.* Five fixed slots in a fixed order told the
   student there were exactly five things to decide. As in the sentence
   builder, the student adds the criteria the report needs, from the
   menu. An omission is then real -- leave out "the other party is a
   customer" and the owner's investment is collected -- and the feedback
   names the event it let in.
2. *The paid-for condition is the first "but not".* A checkbox today,
   a one-off. The cash-basis cost of goods sold task is "change the
   accrual report with one condition", which is the exclude clause with
   one chip: the Report Builder's original demonstration, two reports
   differing by one chip. So Reporting introduces "but not" at that
   step rather than leaving it to Adjusting Entries.
3. *A right report goes into the record.* The reading says a report is
   itself something asserted, and now the record panel shows it: a
   report composed right is recorded as an event, and the difference
   tasks (gross margin, ending inventory) select recorded reports rather
   than compositions held in client state. The same mechanism later
   years use to select what was reported before.
4. *Feedback by difference*, as under Grading, in place of the per-part
   messages.

The check for the rewrite is that the same tasks over the same record
reach the same figures, not that the behavior is the same.

* By lesson

- *Reporting*: chips from groups 1, 2, 3, 6 and 7, "but not" arriving
  with the cash-basis task, plus ending inventory (a "to the report
  date" chip and a difference row over recorded reports).
- *Adjusting Entries*: the exclude clause, groups 4, 5 and 8, the share
  calculations, and the entry derived from the selection. First-year
  problems first; later years once the first-year path is whole.
- *Level 7*: the note's interest promises, falling in the period and
  open at the date.
- *Capstone*: its reports as they are.
- *Later courses*: the full menu, arbitrary periods, group and count,
  several records at once.

* Order of work

1. The criteria vocabulary and the flat-map / full-form shape of a
   recorded report, with the Reporting composer rewritten over them as
   "What changes in Reporting" says; the walkthrough of 2026-09-29
   reaches the same figures. /Done 2026-10-09/ (HANDOFF.md, "The
   selection interface's step 1 is built"). The full form is the flat
   =:reports= map with =:collects= on it; the adapter does not yet turn
   it into the research vocabulary's =collects= / =includes= /
   =excludes=, which can come with step 7.
2. Ending inventory in Reporting. /Done 2026-10-09/: a "on or before
   the report date" chip, a =goods-received-cost= reading, two more
   reports (goods received at cost to date, goods sold at cost to
   date) and ending inventory as their difference; the oracle checks it
   against the batch-by-batch valuation (770 on Harbor Line's year).
3. Extension-based grading with diff feedback, over Reporting's four
   reports first, where the canonical answers are known and tested.
4. The exclude clause, the single-event pick, the share calculations,
   and the derivation reading the selection; the five adjustments over
   first-year records.
5. The records: hire, pay-period work events, paydays; notes with
   interest promises.
6. Lesson and quiz text; =fulfills= to Level 1; Level 7.
7. Later years, hidden records, the full menu.
