#+TITLE: Reports first, then adjusting entries
#+AUTHOR: Matt DeAngelis (with Claude)
#+DATE: 2026-10-09

Supersedes =ACCRUALS-AS-REPORTS.org= (same day), which tried to fix
accruals inside Adjusting Entries.

* The decision (Matt, 2026-10-09)

- *Adjusting entries are a double-entry idea*, and the platform teaches
  double-entry as ACCT 2101 does. The assertive record underneath is for
  downstream uses -- analytics above all -- and to help students tell
  journal-entry classifications apart. The deep differences between
  assertive recording and double-entry reporting are not taught at this
  level. (So the lessons do not, for example, tell students there is "no
  counterparty" when Wages Payable is booked: plainly there is one.)
- *=reports= stands alone.* It selects events already in the record and
  calculates from them. It never carries a =requires=.
- *Every reported figure is a selection and a calculation*, and the
  journal entry is double-entry's form of that calculation.
- *Adjusting entries "top up"* the totals that cannot be read directly
  off the period's events.
- *Reporting comes before Adjusting Entries.* Reporting teaches "select
  the events, total them" over revenue and cost of goods sold; Adjusting
  Entries extends the same move to "select a prior event, calculate this
  period's share".

* Every report: select, then calculate

| Report                          | Select from the record                            | Calculate                                                                   | Double-entry form                                   |
|---------------------------------+---------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------|
| Sales Revenue                   | the sales                                         | add up their amounts                                                        | (the sales' own entries)                            |
| Cost of goods sold              | the sales, and the batches they named             | add up those batches' cost                                                  | (the sales' own entries)                            |
| Ending inventory                | the goods received, and the goods sold            | received at cost, less sold at cost, to the report date                     | (a balance: the inventory accounts)                 |
| Prepaid expense used            | the prepayment                                    | this period's share of its time, at its cost per period                     | DR Expense / CR Prepaid Expense                     |
| Depreciation                    | the equipment's acquisition                       | this period's share of its useful life                                      | DR Depreciation Expense / CR Accumulated Depreciation |
| Wages                           | the labor consumed, and its payment promises      | expense = labor consumed in the period; payable = promises not yet fulfilled | DR Wage Expense / CR Wages Payable (the unpaid top-up) |
| Interest expense                | the note payable                                  | principal x rate x time in this period; payable = the unpaid part           | DR Interest Expense / CR Interest Payable           |
| Interest revenue                | the note receivable                               | the same, the other way                                                     | DR Interest Receivable / CR Interest Revenue        |
| Bad debts                       | the receivables, and their confidences            | the probability-weighted net balance of Accounts Receivable                 | DR Bad Debt Expense / CR Allowance                  |

A wage, an interest charge or a prepaid's use is a number specific to the
report: it is computed as of the report's date, for the report.

* Earlier reports: this period's share

- *The first year* has no earlier reports. The period's share is the
  usage (or time, or wages) from the originating event -- the purchase,
  the hire, the note -- to the report date.
- *Later years* are where earlier reports matter, and there a full
  year's calculation is enough: a full year's share is the same as the
  total elapsed since the originating event, less what earlier reports
  already took (Matt, 2026-10-09).

- *The last year* may be a partial year: the asset's useful life (or the
  prepayment's term) ends partway through. Its share can be calculated
  either as the proportion of the year, or as the remainder: the asset's
  depreciable base less everything reported before. The two agree, and
  the second guarantees the total never exceeds the cost (Matt,
  2026-10-09).

That sameness is the point to teach: these measures spread a cost across
the periods it serves. Practice in later years can show both and let the
student see they agree. Yearly reporting only in 2101 (see Open).

Earlier reports are in the record for later years: the schema already has
a =recorded-report= entity (=:recorded-report/id=,
=:recorded-report/payload=); practice records would carry last year's
reports as events.

* The hire is an event

Hiring an employee becomes an event in the record: a commitment to pay
wages at a rate, until it is ended. Reports reference it.

*Hourly, as an assertive system would record it* (Matt, 2026-10-09):
work events =consumes= hours of labor, and each creates a =requires=
for payment, periodically =fulfilled= by paydays. Then:
- *Wage expense* for the period: total labor consumed in it.
- *Wages payable* at the report date: the =requires= not yet fulfilled.

(A salary would look more like depreciation: time-based, from the hire.)
The hourly model is preferred. Students have not yet been shown how to
consume labor hours -- labor in production was set aside for 2102 -- so
the lessons need a small step for it.

* What it takes

** Lesson order
- [ ] =tutorials/lesson-sequence=: Reporting (9) moves before Adjusting
      Entries (5). Check that Reporting's own reading and record need
      nothing from Adjusting Entries (its year has credit sales,
      collections, a dividend -- no adjustments).
- [ ] The check-ins, retention look-backs and capstone gating follow the
      new order (they read =lesson-sequence=).

** Reporting
- [ ] Add ending inventory (a balance-sheet figure): a new reading, the
      cost of goods received; the task; checked against the batch-by-batch
      valuation the balance check already computes.
- [ ] The reading says, at the start, that every figure on a statement is
      a report: select, then calculate.

** Adjusting Entries, rebuilt
- [ ] Start from the lesson as Matt last edited it (2026-10-09) and
      replace the accrual, prepaid and "reports" sections: each adjustment
      is a report that selects its source event (the prepayment, the
      equipment, the hire, the note) and calculates this period's share.
- [ ] Remove what this decision rules out: =reports= paired with
      =requires=; "no has-counterparty"; "the promise is already in the
      record ... the report gathers it". Quiz question 4 likewise.
- [ ] Problems give the record (source events, and last year's reports
      where needed) and ask for the report: which events, what basis, the
      amount. Graded like Reporting: part by part (selection, basis,
      period), then the figure.
- [ ] The double-entry entry is derived from the report: the debit from
      what is reported, the credit from what the selected event is (a
      prepaid, equipment, the hire's unpaid wages, the note's unpaid
      interest).

** Records
- [ ] Practice companies carry the events reports select: prepayments,
      equipment, hires, notes, and (for later years) earlier reports.
- [ ] Hire events (rate, start date); work events that consume labor hours
      and create a payment promise; paydays that fulfill them.

** Grading and derivation
- [ ] Accrual patterns (=:accrued-wages=, =:accrued-interest-expense=,
      =:interest-revenue-accrual=) and the prepaid and depreciation
      patterns: =reports= alone, with a selection of source events. No
      =requires=.
- [ ] The derivation's accrual rules take the payable/receivable from the
      selected source event, not from a =requires= in the same event.
- [ ] Wages Expense (derivation) vs Wage Expense (patterns, lessons): one
      name.
- [ ] The calculation builder's bases (time-based, systematic allocation,
      accrual, estimation) are the calculation half; the selection half
      is new for these reports (the batch and promise pickers, and
      Reporting's compositions, are the models).

* Decided (Matt, 2026-10-09)

- [X] *Wages*: hourly -- labor consumed creates a payment promise,
      fulfilled on payday; expense is labor consumed, payable is the
      unfulfilled promises. Needs a lesson step on consuming labor hours.
- [X] *Bad debts*: an aggregate over earlier events. Students already
      record confidence with =expects= and were told it feeds bad debt
      expense; the report computes the probability-weighted net balance of
      Accounts Receivable from those confidences.
- [X] *The capstone* gains period-end reports.
- [X] *Quarterly reporting* stays out of the platform while it pilots
      with 2101. When it serves other courses, quarterly and arbitrary
      periods come in -- to show that a report can cover any span.

* Decided, earning an advance (Matt, 2026-10-09)

- [X] *Earning an advance* (Unearned Revenue) is a *delivery*, not a
      report: the delivery is its own event, =provides= the goods and
      =fulfills= the advance's =requires=. "It's not reporting that
      converts unearned revenue; it's delivery." Adjusting Entries' last
      section today records it as =reports= revenue + =fulfills=; the
      rebuild changes it to the delivery.
