#+TITLE: Designs, capacity and printed varieties
#+AUTHOR: Matt DeAngelis (with Claude)
#+DATE: 2026-10-08

* The problem

Level 2 (Production and Transformation) serves nine kinds of problem in
one practice round: turning blanks and ink into printed shirts, printing
with labor, supplies used up, a design bought, a design made in-house, a
capability bought, a service delivered. The intellectual-property cases
sit among the printer and production problems, and a student meets
"bought design: an asset" and "made design: an expense" with nothing
around them to make the difference stand out.

Three further problems surfaced walking through it (2026-10-07):

- A design was offered as an *input* in =allows= ("turn blank shirts and
  ink and Logo Design into printed shirts"). Printing needs the design,
  but does not use it up the way it uses up blanks and ink. Fixed
  2026-10-07: the =allows= input menu lists only things that are used up.
- A bought design required =allows=; a made one did not (it was
  prohibited until 2026-10-07, then optional). Whether the business
  bought the design or made it does not change what it can now do. Only
  the entry differs, because GAAP carries one and expenses the other.
- A design's =allows= named the same output as the printer's: printed
  shirts. The printer prints anything; a design, which costs money to
  produce, adds a /specific/ capacity -- printing that design -- and later
  sales distinguish between prints. The two capacities should not look
  the same.

* Decisions

** The bought design comes first, and mirrors equipment

The purchased intangible is taught first, because it is the same story as
buying a printer: the business pays for something that enables
production, says what it allows, and the record carries it as an asset
-- Design (Intangible Asset), as the printer is Equipment. The design made
in-house is then the special case, and is identified as one: the same
capacity, recorded the same way, and an entry GAAP will not give an
asset, so the designer's wages are an expense.

** =allows= on every design, bought or made

Required on both. The capacity is the economic substance and does not
depend on how the design was acquired. The feedback says plainly what
differs (the entry) and what does not (the capacity). Assertions record
the substance; hints make clear which of them reach the entry.

** A design allows printing /its/ variety

The printer allows turning blanks and ink into printed shirts. A design
allows turning them into printed shirts /of that design/: "Printed
T-Shirts -- Night Owl". Each variety is its own good, with its own
batches and costs, and a sale names the variety it draws from.

- Problems name the design ("the Night Owl design"); students select it,
  as they select the batch a sale came out of.
- Before costly designs are introduced, printed shirts are a plain
  variety: the company's own logo. Production up to that point prints the
  plain variety and needs only the printer.
- A production of a design's variety rests on two things, the printer
  and the design, so it is =is-allowed-by= both. The builder fills both in
  when the transformation matches (see "Capacity, found for the
  student" in =state.cljs=).
